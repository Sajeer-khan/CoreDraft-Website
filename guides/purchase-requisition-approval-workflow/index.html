Purchase Requisition Approval Process: Reduce Procurement Delays

A practical framework for requisitions, delegated approval, RFQs, purchase orders and receiving.

Published: 24 October 2026 · For: business owners and operations teams. This is educational guidance, not legal or professional certification advice.

The question worth asking first

A practical framework for requisitions, delegated approval, RFQs, purchase orders and receiving. What should you see, who should act, and which evidence should survive the handoff? A good process should make those answers easier—not create more bureaucracy.

Why purchasing delays feel invisible

A department requests material, a supervisor asks for more information, procurement contacts suppliers, and finance later learns about the commitment. Everyone may be doing their part, yet nobody can say where the request stands.

Before adopting software, map the journey from need to completed purchase. Separate process steps that genuinely require authorization from routine status updates.

Capture a request that can actually be evaluated

An effective requisition states what is needed, the quantity or estimated scope, when it is needed, the requesting unit, intended cost center or project, and relevant specifications. Supporting files should answer predictable questions before the request enters an approval queue.

Distinguish urgent requests from incomplete requests. An urgent label should not eliminate essential controls; instead define what expedited routing requires and how exceptions will be documented.

Make approval authority explicit

Decide which roles can approve which types of spend and what happens when an approver is unavailable. Consider segregation of duties for high-value purchases, but avoid unnecessary loops for small routine items.

A useful queue identifies the responsible person, submission date, decision required and current status. Notifications can help, but they should complement—not replace—a reliable in-app record.

Link supplier selection to the purchase record

An RFQ should make supplier responses comparable on relevant terms, not only unit price. Delivery lead time, specification compliance, warranty and payment conditions may matter. Record the basis for a selection so a later reviewer can understand it.

Purchase orders should remain connected to approved requests and supplier quotations. When goods arrive, the receipt record should reference the order and any discrepancy instead of becoming an unrelated note.

Watch the operational signals

Useful measures include waiting approvals, overdue supplier responses, open purchase orders, partial receipts and invoices lacking appropriate receiving evidence. Decide who will respond to each type of exception.

For a tool demonstration, test one normal requisition and one messy scenario: a revised specification, a partial delivery or a rejected approval. That tells you more about operational fit than a long feature checklist.

A three-question diagnostic

  1. Who owns the request while it is waiting?
  2. When does an RFQ need formal comparison?
  3. How does receiving connect to the original requirement?

If the answers depend entirely on one individual's memory or private inbox, the workflow is a useful candidate for clearer shared records.
